Figure 2 | Nucleus segmentation and the deep-learning boundary. (a) Instance F1 (IoU 0.5) of twelve methods on BBBC039 (n=100 images); bars ranked, 95% bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples of per-image scores); fluorostats blue, deep-learning tools in colour, classical thresholds grey. (b) Forest plot vs validated DL (StarDist, Cellpose, Omnipose); paired differences exclude zero. (c) Representative BBBC039 crops: raw, expert ground truth (green outlines), fluorostats (blue outlines); scale bar 20 µm (BBBC039 ~0.31 µm/px). (d) Instance F1 vs nuclear overlap (BBBC024 c00-c75): all non-DL methods (grey; fluorostats blue) collapse while DL (dashed) holds ~0.96; red band = DL advantage. (e) fluorostats overlay on a well-separated (c00) and a crowded (c75) field, showing connected-component merging under overlap. (f) Data-anchored scope map: fluorostats is at parity with trained segmenters up to ~40% crowding (green), beyond which a trained instance segmenter is preferred (red).
